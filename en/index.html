Why does TrustiScore exist?

In the digital age, we rely on apps and online services for everything: communication, work, leisure. But do we really know where our personal data ends up?

The stakes are high: most non-European providers (Big Tech first and foremost) store our information outside the EU, under extraterritorial laws such as the US CLOUD Act (forced access to data by US authorities, even when it is hosted in Europe). The result? We lose control over our photos, messages, histories...

See the grades of all apps →

But first: what is digital sovereignty?

Digital sovereignty is the ability of a state, a service, a person, an app and so on to keep legal, technical and economic control over its own data, without excessive dependence on foreign powers.

For Europe, this means complying with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), keeping data in the EU, and limiting the risk of foreign regulations such as the CLOUD Act applying…

Sounds complicated? Don't worry: TrustiScore makes it simple for you!

The method: how the grade is calculated

We assess each app on 5 key criteria, scored from 0 to 4 points (weighted into a score out of 100 and converted to A-E). This rigorous method is directly inspired by European standards (SecNumCloud – the French cybersecurity agency's cloud qualification – and ISO 27001).

The 5 criteria, from heaviest to lightest

Data Location

25 % percent of the score

Governance and Jurisdiction

20 % percent of the score

Technical Control

20 % percent of the score

Compliance and Certifications

20 % percent of the score

Transparency

15 % percent of the score

The result: a scale from A to E

The thresholds are public: A from 80 points out of 100, B from 60, C from 40, D from 20, E below that. The grade is not a black box.

How to use it

Don't panic, it's simple:

  1. Search for an app or service (e.g. Tuta Mail, ChatGPT, Vinted…).
  2. See its TrustiScore: an overall grade from A to E, with coloured badges (A = excellent, E = risky).
  3. Dive into the details: full explanations and the reasoning behind the grade.
  4. Consider an alternative: suggestions of apps that better respect digital sovereignty, with their TrustiScore.

How the project started

It all began with a personal challenge: building a smartphone "purged" as far as possible of Big Tech apps. After extensive testing, the idea came up to take the experience further by sharing it: why not create an app that objectively rates the "digital sovereignty" of other apps?

A good deal of hard work later, here is TrustiScore: an open source app, meant to grow with the community. Our mission? To help you choose tools that respect your privacy and European sovereignty.

Our commitment

TrustiScore is committed to providing independent, objective and transparent assessments. We are not affiliated with any company and receive no payment from the services we assess.

Our principles

  1. Full independence: no conflict of interest with the companies assessed
  2. Objectivity: assessments based only on verifiable facts
  3. Transparency: a public methodology and detailed explanations for every grade
  4. Continuous updates: regular reassessment to keep up with how services change
  5. Respect for privacy: we practise what we preach — see what we collect
